# APEC / ASEAN / Thailand — Regional Architecture

The institutional and infrastructural detail that matters for ASEAN-based or ASEAN-adjacent practitioners. Read whenever a question is regional, cross-border within Asia-Pacific, or Thailand-specific.

## APEC (Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation)

21 member economies; ~61% of global GDP; ~46% of global trade. Founded 1989, headquartered in Singapore. Forum, not a binding FTA — operates by "concerted unilateralism" and consensus.

### Active instruments (2025–2026)
- **Supply Chain Connectivity Framework Action Plan III (SCFAP III, 2022–2026)** — addresses five chokepoints: (1) inefficient digitalisation, (2) inadequate infrastructure development, (3) insufficient cooperation on data flows and cross-border payments, (4) lack of understanding on green supply chains, (5) lack of targeted MSME support. Mid-term review (2024) showed progress on chokepoints 1–4, mixed on 5.
- **APEC Connectivity Blueprint (2015–2025)** — physical, institutional, people-to-people connectivity. Final review concluding 2026.
- **APEC Centre of Excellence for Paperless Trade (ACCEPT)** — established 2025, housed at Singapore Business Federation; Australian co-funding. Capacity building for paperless cross-border trade.
- **APEC Business Travel Card (ABTC)** — visa-free business travel; virtual ABTC rolling out across members.
- **AI for Supply Chains** — Korea-led project launching 2026 (follows the 2025 "AI for Trade" initiative).
- **Putrajaya Vision 2040** — long-range strategic frame.

### Hosts
2025: Korea (Gyeongju). 2026: China. 2027: Vietnam. 2028: Mexico. 2030: Singapore.

## ASEAN (10 economies, AEC since 2015)

### ATIGA — ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement
- ~99% of intra-ASEAN tariff lines at 0% for ASEAN-6 (Brunei, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand)
- CLMV (Cambodia, Lao, Myanmar, Vietnam) substantially complete with phase-in flexibilities
- **Form D** is the certificate of origin under ATIGA; **e-Form D** is the digitized version exchanged via ASW
- Key origin rule: RVC ≥ 40% or change in tariff heading (CTH); product-specific rules in Annex 3

### ASEAN Single Window (ASW)
- Live operation since December 2019 (all 10 member states)
- Currently exchanging:
  - **e-Form D** (ATIGA certificate of origin)
  - **ACDD** (ASEAN Customs Declaration Document)
  - **e-Phyto** certificates (Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand fully exchanging; Malaysia in implementation)
- ASW e-Form D issuance grew from 500K (2019) to 1M+ (2022)
- Cumulative savings reported at USD 6.4B+ and ~4 days reduction in transit time per transaction
- "Next Generation ASW" study underway to extend connectivity beyond ASEAN to dialogue partners

### ASEAN Customs Transit System (ACTS)
- Single declaration for goods moving overland through multiple AMS
- Enables a Bangkok→HCMC truck movement under one customs document instead of multiple national filings
- Adoption uneven; useful for contract manufacturers shipping intermediate goods regionally

### RCEP — Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership
- In force January 2022
- ASEAN-10 + China + Japan + Korea + Australia + New Zealand
- World's largest FTA by GDP and population (~30% of global GDP, ~30% of population)
- **Full cumulation** rules of origin — inputs from any RCEP party count toward originating status
- For some product categories, RCEP outperforms ATIGA on landed cost when inputs come from CN/JP/KR
- Form RCEP-COO is the certificate of origin

### ASEAN+1 FTAs (the supplementary stack)
- ACFTA (ASEAN-China), 2010 — Form E
- AKFTA (ASEAN-Korea), 2007 — Form AK
- AJCEP (ASEAN-Japan), 2008 — Form AJ
- AIFTA (ASEAN-India), 2010 — Form AI
- AANZFTA (ASEAN-Australia/NZ), 2010 — Form AANZ

### CPTPP — Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership
- 11 members; ASEAN members in: Singapore, Malaysia, Brunei, Vietnam
- Thailand has expressed interest (politically contested domestically)
- Higher-standard rules than RCEP; deeper services and IP commitments

### GMS — Greater Mekong Subregion
- Functional grouping (CN-MM-LA-TH-KH-VN), ADB-coordinated since 1992
- **GMS CBTA** (Cross-Border Transport Agreement) — overland transport coordination; implementation uneven
- Three economic corridors:
  - **NSEC** (North-South) — Kunming → Chiang Khong/Boten → Bangkok → Laem Chabang
  - **EWEC** (East-West) — Mawlamyine → Mae Sot → Mukdahan → Da Nang
  - **SEC** (Southern) — Bangkok → Phnom Penh → Ho Chi Minh City
- Mae Sot–Myawaddy bridge has been disrupted multiple times since 2021 due to Myanmar political situation

## Thailand — practitioner detail

### Customs system
- **e-Customs since 1 January 2007** — paperless declarations covering e-Import, e-Export, e-Manifest, e-Payment, e-Warehouse
- All commercial importers must register and obtain a **paperless license** (customs card), valid 3 years, mandatory for e-Customs access
- Digital certificate required for identity verification
- Risk management system applies risk profiling; AEO-certified entities get lower inspection rates
- **AEO program** — tiered (importer/exporter/broker); MRAs with Korea, Hong Kong, more in progress

### Tax structure on imports
- **Duty**: 0–80% (average ~21%); 0% on many lines under FTAs
- **VAT**: 7% on (CIF + Duty + Excise + other duties + fees) — **first-baht rule** since 2024 (no de minimis on commercial imports)
- **Excise**: applies on alcohol, tobacco, fuel, vehicles, luxury goods (rates vary)

### Promotion regimes
- **BOI (Board of Investment)** — sector- and activity-based promotion; common privileges include CIT exemption (3–8 years), duty exemption on machinery, duty exemption on raw materials for export production, foreign land ownership for manufacturing zones
- **IEAT** (Industrial Estate Authority of Thailand) — operates industrial estates and free zones
- **EEC** (Eastern Economic Corridor) — Chonburi, Rayong, Chachoengsao; targets 12 priority industries (next-gen automotive, smart electronics, advanced agri/biotech, biofuels/biochemicals, robotics, aviation/logistics, medical hub, digital, defense, education/HRD); enhanced CIT, smart visa, land ownership, faster permits
- **EECi/EECd** — Innovation and Digital sub-zones with R&D-targeted privileges

### Major nodes
- **Laem Chabang Port (LCB)** — primary deep-sea container port, ~9M+ TEU/year, Chonburi Province; gateway to Europe, China, Americas
- **Bangkok Port (Klong Toey)** — river port, smaller draft, mainly ASEAN feeder
- **Map Ta Phut** — petrochemical and industrial bulk; Rayong Province; key for upstream/downstream energy logistics (PTT/PTTEP-relevant)
- **Suvarnabhumi (BKK)** — primary air freight gateway; cold chain capability
- **Don Mueang (DMK)** — secondary, mainly LCC; some freight
- **ICDs** — Lat Krabang (largest ICD, supports rail-shuttle to LCB); Latrabang–LCB rail corridor a key inland-to-port mode
- **U-Tapao** — being expanded as third commercial airport / aerotropolis under EEC plan

### Laem Chabang Transshipment Sandbox (current)
- Notification 213/2568 (2025), effective 1 December 2025 → 30 November 2026
- Simplifies documentation for vessel transshipment declaration numbers
- Implements electronic process — Thailand is currently the only ASEAN member with electronic transshipment declaration
- One-year trial; could be institutionalized and become an ASEAN reference if successful
- Strategic context: positions LCB to compete with Singapore and Port Klang for transshipment volumes from CLMV

### Trade and customs brokers
- Licensed by Thai Customs; AEO-certified brokers get expedited treatment
- TIFFA (Thai International Freight Forwarders Association) is the industry body
- Major international forwarders all operational (DHL, K+N, DSV, DB Schenker, Yusen, NX/NYK, Maersk Logistics, etc.)
- Domestic 3PL: SCG Logistics, Kerry Logistics (now SF), JWD (now SCGJWD), Linfox

### Practical implications for Bangkok-based planning
- Default to **Vietnam–Thailand–Mexico/India** triangulation in scenario planning rather than treating it as exotic
- Assume freight-cost volatility from Red Sea and Taiwan-Strait risk; multi-mode contingency on critical lanes
- The **EEC + LCB Sandbox + ASW** stack is genuinely differentiated in ASEAN; treat it as a strategic asset, not bureaucracy
- Watch the Laem Chabang vs Singapore/Port Klang transshipment-hub competition through 2026–2030 — outcomes will reshape ASEAN logistics network design
- For overland intra-ASEAN flows under 1500km, GMS road corridors are competitive with sea+feeder when the customs/transit overhead is contained (ACTS helps)
