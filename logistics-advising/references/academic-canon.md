# Academic Canon — Working Bibliography

The named contributions still load-bearing in modern logistics practice. Cite by author/year when grounding a claim or pushing back against vendor framing.

## Inventory & ordering theory
- **Harris (1913)** — *Operations and Cost*, Factory The Magazine of Management. The Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) formula. Q* = √(2DS/H). Foundation of every reorder-point system; modern WMS reorder rules descend from it.
- **Edgeworth (1888); Arrow, Harris, Marschak (1951)** — Newsvendor problem. Single-period stocking under demand uncertainty with overage and underage costs. Foundation of modern safety-stock math.
- **Arrow, Karlin, Scarf (1958)** — *Studies in the Mathematical Theory of Inventory and Production*. Base-stock and (s,S) policies under continuous review.
- **Clark, Scarf (1960)** — Multi-echelon inventory theory. Foundation for DRP and modern allocation engines.

## Demand variance and the structural causes of supply-chain instability
- **Forrester (1961)** — *Industrial Dynamics*, MIT Press. Demand variance amplification upstream — what later became "the bullwhip effect".
- **Lee, Padmanabhan, Whang (1997)** — "The Bullwhip Effect in Supply Chains", *Sloan Management Review*. Formalized the four causes: demand-signal processing, order batching, price fluctuation, rationing/shortage gaming. Still the canonical diagnosis.

## Routing and transportation optimization
- **Dantzig, Ramser (1959)** — "The Truck Dispatching Problem", *Management Science*. The Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP). Modern variants: CVRP (capacitated), VRPTW (time windows), VRPPD (pickup and delivery), DVRP (dynamic).
- **Linear programming foundations** — Dantzig (1947) simplex method; underpins all modern network optimization.

## Resilience, agility, and supply-chain strategy
- **Hau Lee (2004)** — "The Triple-A Supply Chain", *Harvard Business Review*. Agile, Adaptable, Aligned. Predates COVID by 16 years; described the resilience playbook the industry then "discovered" in 2020.
- **Sheffi (2005)** — *The Resilient Enterprise*, MIT Press. Disruption taxonomy and recovery framework.
- **Christopher (1992; ongoing editions)** — *Logistics and Supply Chain Management*. Time-based competition; agile vs. lean trade-offs.

## Process taxonomy and lean
- **Ohno (1978)** — *Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production*. The seven mudas; pull replenishment; kanban.
- **Shingo (1985)** — *A Revolution in Manufacturing: The SMED System*. Single-minute exchange of die; setup-time reduction enabling small-batch flow.
- **Goldratt (1984)** — *The Goal*. Theory of Constraints; bottleneck-driven scheduling.
- **Womack, Jones, Roos (1990)** — *The Machine That Changed the World*. Coined "lean production" for Western audiences.
- **Supply Chain Council (1996), now ASCM/APICS** — SCOR model: Plan / Source / Make / Deliver / Return / Enable.

## Container and logistics history (for context, not prescription)
- **Levinson (2006)** — *The Box: How the Shipping Container Made the World Smaller and the World Economy Bigger*. McLean's 1956 Sea-Land voyage; standardization as the productivity unlock.
- **Drucker (1962)** — "The Economy's Dark Continent", *Fortune*. Famously called distribution "the most sadly neglected, most promising area of American business".

## Finance, working capital, and metrics
- **APICS (now ASCM) Operations Management Body of Knowledge** — DIO/DSO/DPO and Cash-to-Cash standardization.
- **Gartner Supply Chain Top 25** (annual) — methodology weights both financial outcomes (ROA, revenue growth, inventory turns) and ESG/community metrics. The canonical industry leaderboard.
- **CSCMP State of Logistics Report** (annual) — US-centric but widely cited industry cost-of-logistics benchmarks.

## How to use this in practice
- When pushing back against vendor framing ("agentic AI eliminates the need for forecasts"), cite Forrester/Lee — bullwhip causes are structural, not solvable by tooling alone
- When recommending safety-stock changes, ground in newsvendor logic
- When discussing cross-functional resilience, Lee 2004 Triple-A pre-empts most "post-COVID rethink" arguments
- When discussing routing optimization, name VRP variants accurately (CVRP vs VRPTW matters)
- When the user asks "what's the theoretical basis for X", point to the canon; don't invent
